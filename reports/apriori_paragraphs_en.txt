Parallel Apriori used here is the Count Distribution method of Agrawal and Shafer (1996). Each kept sale is one basket. The items are property type (detached, semi-detached, terraced, flat; Other is dropped), tenure (freehold or leasehold; Commonhold is not a column), a separate new-build flag, and a low, mid, or high price band. The price cuts are tertiles of log price on the rows drawn for that size. The same minimum support 0.05, confidence 0.45, and lift 1.15 are used, and itemsets stop at size 3. Candidate itemsets are built on the driver from the previous frequent itemsets. Every engine then counts those candidates on its own partition of the baskets and the counts are summed. This is separate from Lloyd k-means. The k=2 chosen for clustering is not an Apriori parameter.

The method is used because the project compares parallel engines on the same Price Paid file, not because the association rules are a London or macroeconomic result. Apriori (Agrawal and Srikant, 1994, VLDB) finds the frequent itemsets. Count Distribution (Agrawal and Shafer, 1996, IEEE Transactions on Knowledge and Data Engineering 8(6), 962–969) keeps the baskets in place and adds the local counts. A MapReduce Apriori uses one job per round (Lin, Lee and Hsueh, 2012, ICUIMC). Python keeps the counts in memory across six processes. Spark local[*] caches the baskets between rounds. It is not a YARN job.

On the full file the strongest rule is leasehold and high price implying flat: support 0.0524, confidence 0.8617, lift 4.6918. The reverse, flat and high price implying leasehold, has support 0.0524, confidence 0.9833, and lift 4.1727.
